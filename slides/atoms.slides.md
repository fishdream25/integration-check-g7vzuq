# Atoms
## How light becomes chemical energy
First-year university biology | Alembic demo

<!-- slide -->
## Why photosynthesis matters
- Converts light energy into chemical energy in organic bonds
- Performed by plants, algae, and cyanobacteria
- Releases oxygen as a byproduct of water splitting
- Supplies most biological energy for complex life
- Helps maintain Earth's atmospheric oxygen content

<!-- @notes -->
Start by asking students where the energy in their last meal originally came from.

<!-- slide -->
## Two linked stages
- Light-dependent reactions capture light and split water
- The Calvin cycle fixes carbon into sugars
- The first stage produces ATP and NADPH
- The second stage uses ATP and NADPH

<!-- slide -->
## Capturing light energy
- Chlorophyll absorbs mainly red and blue light
- Accessory pigments widen the absorbed spectrum
- Energy passes to reaction centres in photosystems
- Photosystems II and I drive electron transport

<!-- slide -->
## Water splitting releases oxygen
- Photosystem II oxidises water molecules
- Electrons replace those lost from chlorophyll
- Oxygen is released as a byproduct

$$
\ce{2H2O -> O2 + 4H+ + 4e-}
$$

<!-- slide 2col -->
## Energy carriers and carbon fixation
<!-- @left -->
**Light-dependent outputs**
- Electron transport and chemiosmosis make ATP
- Electron carriers reduce NADP$^+$ to NADPH
<!-- @right -->
**Calvin cycle inputs**
- RuBisCO fixes CO$_2$ onto a five-carbon sugar
- ATP and NADPH reduce the product to triose phosphate

## Storage and respiration
- Triose phosphates are assembled into glucose, sucrose, and starch
- Cells oxidise stored carbohydrates during cellular respiration
- The overall processes are approximately reverse reactions

$$
\ce{6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2}
$$

<!-- @notes -->
Ask students to identify which reactants and products are reversed in respiration.
